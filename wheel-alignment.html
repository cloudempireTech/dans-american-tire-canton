Why Alignment Matters

One Pothole Can Ruin a $1,000 Set of Tires

Hitting a pothole on West Tuscarawas or catching a curb during parallel parking can knock your steering angles out of factory specification by fractions of an inch. Even a tenth-of-a-degree misalignment causes tires to drag sideways down the highway, scrubbing off rubber and slashing tire lifespan by up to 50%.

At Dan’s American Tire, our technicians utilize Hunter Hawkeye multi-camera optical alignment equipment. High-resolution digital imaging targets clamp safely onto your wheels without metal-to-metal contact, measuring Camber, Caster, and Toe in real time against manufacturer tolerances.

Signs You Need an Immediate Alignment:

  • Vehicle pulls noticeably to the left or right on flat highway roads
  • Steering wheel is crooked or off-center while driving straight
  • Rapid or feathered wear patterns along the inside or outside tire edge
  • Vibrations or loose wander in the steering wheel at highway speeds
Chassis Geometry

The Three Angles We Calibrate to Perfection

Understanding the physics behind your vehicle’s contact patch and steering stability.

Camber Angle Calibration

Camber is the inward or outward tilt of the tires when viewed from the front. Incorrect camber concentrates vehicle weight onto only one side of the tread, destroying the shoulder in just a few thousand miles.

Caster Angle Diagnostics

Caster is the forward or rearward slope of your steering axis. Proper caster ensures self-centering steering when exiting turns and maintains rock-solid high-speed tracking on windy Ohio highways.

Toe Angle Adjustment

Toe is the direction tires point relative to the centerline. Even slight toe-out or toe-in produces a continuous dragging effect akin to sandpapering your tires against the pavement on every commute.

Comprehensive Standard

Full Suspension Health Check Included

An alignment cannot hold if your suspension components have excessive play. Before putting your vehicle on the Hunter alignment rack, our ASE-certified mechanics inspect:

  • Tie Rod Ends & Steering Rack Play: Checking inner and outer joints for looseness or torn rubber grease boots.
  • Upper & Lower Ball Joints: Inspecting structural pivot joints supporting your wheels under suspension load.
  • Control Arm Bushings: Checking for cracked or perished elastomer bushings causing wheel shift under braking.
